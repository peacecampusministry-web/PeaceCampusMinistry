What is campus ministry?

A community on campus where students can find friendship, ask honest questions, and explore faith.

Read the article

Peace Campus Ministry supports students in both their academic and spiritual journeys. In the midst of busy schedules and daily pressures, students can find a place to stay rooted in faith through prayer, worship, Bible study, and fellowship. Here, faith is nurtured, friendships are strengthened, and lives are transformed by the love of Christ.

Our community is primarily composed of international students from the University of Houston who gather to grow spiritually, experience a sense of belonging, and serve fellow students. Students can take part in meaningful discussions, learn from one another’s perspectives, and deepen their connection with faith in a safe and welcoming space.

By following Jesus’ teachings, we encourage students to develop a strong moral compass and a sense of responsibility toward fellow students and the wider community. You can start simply: come to a gathering, meet someone new, and see if this community feels like a place for you.

Faith & tradition · 4 min read

A first look at the Anglican Church

Ancient roots, shared worship, and a faith that makes space for thoughtful questions.

Read the article

Anglican Christians belong to a global family of churches with historic roots in the Church of England and early British Christianity. The Anglican Communion today includes 42 self-governing provinces in more than 165 countries. The Anglican Church in North America (ACNA), founded in 2009, is one distinct Anglican province in North America; Anglican churches around the world are connected in a communion rather than governed by one central authority.

Anglican worship is liturgical: Scripture, prayers, hymns, and sacraments shape a shared pattern of worship, often guided by a Book of Common Prayer. The first English Book of Common Prayer was published in 1549 and is associated with Thomas Cranmer. The service commonly brings together the Word—Bible readings, preaching, and prayer—and the Sacrament of Holy Communion. Baptism and Communion are central sacraments, and the Apostles’ and Nicene Creeds summarize core Christian beliefs such as the Trinity, the incarnation of Jesus Christ, salvation through faith, and the life of the universal Church. Anglican churches are historically led by bishops.

For students, this tradition can offer a steady rhythm of prayer, worship, Bible study, mentorship, and Communion while making room to explore questions of faith, reason, and life in a diverse campus setting. Science helps us study the natural world; faith also invites questions about purpose, meaning, and how we live. Our ministry encourages spiritual formation through prayer, worship, Scripture, mentoring, and service. We seek to share our Christian faith with clarity and to meet people of other beliefs with respect, humility, and a desire for understanding and cooperation.

You don’t need to know the responses or understand every tradition before you visit. You’re welcome to observe, join in as you feel comfortable, and ask questions afterward. Explore Anglican history or learn about the Anglican Church in North America.

Bible study · Everyone welcome

Read the Bible with us

No prior experience needed—just bring your curiosity and whatever questions you have.

Read the post

Bible study is a chance to slow down, read a passage together, and notice what it might say about God and everyday life. We share observations, ask questions, and listen to one another. There’s no quiz and no expectation that you already know the Bible.

Bring a Bible if you have one; we’ll have copies to share. You’re welcome to speak up, take it all in quietly, or come with a friend. We’ll announce the next study at our weekly gathering—message us for the current time and location.

Faith in daily life

Growing in faith, learning, and service

Anglican student ministry connects spiritual formation with the questions and responsibilities of campus life.

Read the post

University life raises big questions about knowledge, purpose, and meaning. Science helps us understand the natural world; Christian faith explores questions of purpose, hope, and how we care for one another. Students are invited to think carefully, ask honest questions, and grow in faith without treating learning as something to fear.

Spiritual growth takes shape through regular prayer, worship, Bible study, mentoring, and the Eucharist. Students are also encouraged to serve through community involvement and service-learning, putting compassion and care for neighbors into practice.

Through Anglican Peace Campus Ministry (APCM) in the Greater Houston area, we seek to understand people of other faiths and build relationships with religious communities and places of worship. We share our Christian faith openly while looking for common ground, focusing on shared values and similarities with respect for our differences. These conversations have helped many fruitful relationships grow.
